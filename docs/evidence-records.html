Evidence records

The process records DO-178C asks for, and how Noyce IDE keeps each one honest - the roster, problem reports, CCB meetings, change sets, MISRA deviations, reviews, SQA and CM records, tool qualification and parameter data.

Evidence Records holds everything a certification authority asks you to show about how the software was built: who did what, which problems were raised and decided, which changes were authorised, and which reviews happened. Open it from its tile or Noyce Compliance: Open Evidence Records.

Two rules apply to every tab:

  • Records that describe something that happened are append-only. Reviews, CCB minutes and SQA / CM records can be added but never edited; a mistaken SQA / CM record is voided with a reason and stays on file.
  • Each form tells you whether the record will count. A box under the form says either "This record is complete and will count toward its objective" or lists why it will not count yet.

Note

In v2.0.7, checks in this view that depend on the software level, such as admissible verification cases and tool qualification levels, are evaluated at Level A.

Start with the roster#

Roster lists the people who may author, review, approve, sign and attend meetings. Fill it in before anything else.

  1. Select Add person.
  2. Enter an Id (an employee id or email a record can cite), a Name and the Organisation.
  3. Choose one or more roles: developer, verifier, sqa, configuration-manager, manager, certification-liaison.
  4. Select Save.

The roster refuses entries that would weaken the records: a person with no id or no name, a person with no role, a duplicate id, and any name that identifies an AI or a tool rather than a person.

The roster decides:

  • whether a review is independent,
  • who may verify that a problem report is closed,
  • who may attend, chair and act as SQA at a CCB meeting, and whether it was quorate,
  • who may approve a change set or a MISRA deviation,
  • who may make SQA and configuration-management records.

Limitation

Remove deletes a person immediately, without asking for confirmation.

Problem reports#

Defects, deficiencies and change requests, with their disposition. Open problem reports are what authorise a code change.

Select New report and fill in the Title, Raised by, Classification (requirement, design, code, verification, process or documentation), Severity and Description. An open blocking report stops a baseline.

Lifecycle: open → in-work → resolved, deferred or closed. As a report moves on, more fields become required:

To It needs
Resolved A disposition: fix, defer or no-change.
Deferred A rationale, a defer disposition, the safety effect, an AC/AMC 20-189 certification class, and a CCB decision to defer. A significant report cannot be deferred past certification.
Closed A disposition, the certification class, the CCB decision, who implemented it, the changed configuration items for a fix, and Closure verified by: a person on the roster who is neither the implementer nor the person who dispositioned it.

A closed or deferred report that fails these checks is not saved. Saved reports that later become inconsistent are badged incoherent in the list.

CCB#

Change Control Board meetings and their decisions. A decision approves, rejects or defers the problem reports and change requests it names.

  1. Select Record a meeting.
  2. Set the Meeting id, Date, the Attendees present, the Chair and the SQA (both chosen from the attendees).
  3. Add decision for each item: the subjects (for example PR-001, CR-002), the outcome (approve, reject or defer), the rationale, and a revisit date for a deferral.
  4. Write the Minutes and select Append minutes.

A meeting is quorate with at least three attendees including a chair and an SQA representative, and the badge says quorum or why not. Minutes are appended, never rewritten, and are refused if a decision names no subject, has no rationale, or was taken without quorum.

Change sets#

Code changes proposed by agents and fixers, waiting for authority, gates and approval. They are created by Fix with Noyce, the CBMC and CodeQL fixers, the Firmware Validation Loop and Orchestrator agents, not in this tab.

The full workflow is in Review and apply AI changes. The authority you cite must be an open or in-work problem report, a change request approved by a quorate CCB decision, or that CCB decision itself. Until you select Generate portable build, the build gate is skipped.

MISRA deviations#

Approved departures from a MISRA rule, with their scope and justification. Findings a deviation covers are counted as covered in the static-analysis results.

Select New deviation, or Import from docs to read docs/misra-deviations.md and add each entry as proposed. Each deviation records the rule (or *), category, title, the files and lines or vendor folders it covers, the rationale, and a risk assessment with controls. Choose an Approver from the roster and select Approve.

A mandatory guideline cannot be deviated, and an approver must be a person on the roster, never an AI. Editing an approved deviation sends it back to proposed.

Reviews#

Records of reviews that took place, with their checklist. Reviews are what close most Annex A objectives.

Select Record a review, choose the Target (plan, standard, requirements, design, code, verification cases, verification results or configuration), name the item, the checklist, the Author of the item and the Reviewer, answer each checklist item, and set the outcome: accepted, accepted with actions, or rejected. Accepted with actions needs the problem reports it raised. Select Append.

Independence is worked out, not ticked. A review is not independent when the reviewer or the author is an AI, when it is a self-review, when there is no roster, or when either person is not on it. The badge says independent or not independent with the reason.

SQA / CM / liaison#

Software quality assurance, configuration management and certification liaison records. Each kind closes specific objectives, but only when made by someone holding the matching role.

Kind Role needed Closes
SQA audit sqa A-9 (1–4), A-1 (6)
Conformity review sqa A-9 (5)
Configuration index configuration-manager A-8 (1)
Release / archive configuration-manager A-8 (4)
Load control configuration-manager A-8 (5)
Environment control configuration-manager A-8 (6)
Certification liaison certification-liaison A-10 (1–3)

Select New record (it needs a roster), pick the kind, the person, the date, what it covers and a summary, then Record. Records are not edited; Void marks a mistaken one with who voided it and why.

Tool qualification#

The tool register: every tool used, the credit taken for it, and whether it needs qualification.

Seed register adds the tools Noyce IDE uses (the IDE itself, compiler, linker, CBMC, CodeQL, llvm-cov, the Unity runner and cppcheck), each with no credit taken. New tool adds another. For each, record the version, the executable's SHA-256, the credit taken (none, verification or development), the DO-330 criterion and, whenever credit is taken, the rationale.

Qualification is required when credit is taken and the tool's output is not independently verified. The badge then shows the tool qualification level (TQL-1 to TQL-5) from the criterion and the software level, and asks for the qualification data.

Verification cases and parameter data#

Verification cases describe each test, analysis, review or inspection: the requirements it verifies, normal-range or robustness, the environment (target, simulator or host; only a target result discharges A-6(5)), the procedure, the expected result and, at Levels A and B, a reviewer other than the author.

Parameter data lists each parameter data item with its file, SHA-256, description, requirements and how it was verified. A project with none can declare No parameter data with a statement and a plan reference.
